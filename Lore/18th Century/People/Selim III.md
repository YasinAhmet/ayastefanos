## Summary

Sultan Selim III (reigned 1789–1807) was the first of the reforming sultans of the modern era. Note that the books often write 'Sultan Selim' for [[Yavuz Sultan Selim]] (Selim I), and many passages in the mentions list refer to him instead.

[[Hafız Hakkı Paşa]]'s diary makes him the start of the Ottoman 'century-long history of reform': 'In the time of Sultan Selim III we began reform with the barracks. The Selimiye barracks still stand in all their majesty on the Marmara shore.' [[Ahmet Emin Yalman]] writes that Selim was deposed because of his new ideals. He spent the rest of his life in something like captivity, passing those ideals on to his young cousin, the future [[Mahmud II]].

In foreign affairs, the video transcripts note that Sultan Selim agreed to peace after a Russian victory, which confirmed Russia's hold on the Black Sea and Crimea ([[Russian annexation of Crimea (1783)]], [[Catherine the Great]]). Several books cite Uriel Heyd's study of the ulema and Westernisation under Selim III and Mahmud II.
