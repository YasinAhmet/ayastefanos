## Summary

Peter the Great, Tsar of Russia (1672–1725, per Akşin), is the subject of Robert Massie's biography, a source in this vault (American view). In March 1711 he declared war on the Ottomans and called on the Balkan peoples to rise with Russia (Mantran). He was surrounded at the Prut and forced to make peace (see [[Prut Campaign (1711)]]). Ortaylı says the Porte scorned him in 1711 but had changed its view by 1730. Turks called him 'Deli Petro' (Mad Peter), and Ortaylı quotes a Russian writer that the Turks judged his work best. Tonguç recalls stories of his harsh reforms. Cox (American view) says Russia had aimed at [[İstanbul]] since his time. Akşin asks whether 1908 was a Peter-style 'enlightened despotism' rather than a modern revolution.
