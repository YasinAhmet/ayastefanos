# Soul of the Age (1873–1919)

> [!warning] Fictional protocol
> This note is built from **novels and plays** (see [[Rules]]). It describes **how the age felt**: its moods, manners, sounds, money worries, loves and fears. It is **not a source of historical fact**. Nothing here is used as evidence anywhere else in the vault. For facts, follow the links to the historical notes.

**Sources used (all fiction):**
- [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)|Namık Kemal, *Vatan Yahut Silistre*]], a play first staged in 1873
- [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)|Namık Kemal, *İntibah*]], a novel written 1873–76
- [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)|Şinasi, *Şair Evlenmesi*]], a play from 1860, the voice of the generation just before
- [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)|Mizancı Murat, *Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı?*]], a novel from 1890
- [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)|Mehmet Rauf, *Halas*]], a novel from 1929 set in 1918–20
- [[Allahın Süngüleri Reis Paşa (Attilâ İlhan)|Attilâ İlhan, *Allahın Süngüleri: Reis Paşa*]], a novel from 2002 set in 1920–21, just past the end of the period

## 1. The feeling in one paragraph

The age sounds like steamboat whistles on the Bosphorus, the evening call to prayer, and the cry of "Padişahım çok yaşa!" ("Long live the Sultan!") rising over cannon fire. Educated young men want to be *alafranga*: they read French, play the piano and go to the theatre in Beyoğlu, while the neighbourhood watches them with suspicion. Marriage is still arranged by matchmakers and go-betweens. Enslaved women are still bought and sold, even in the finest houses. Money drains away, old households collapse into debt, and a clerk ends up writing petitions for a few coins. Above everything hangs a new, dangerous word: ***vatan*** (homeland). It was cheered on stage in 1873, and people were sent into exile for it. By 1918–19 the mood has turned to humiliation. Foreign warships lie in the harbour, flags of the victors fill the streets, and a character hears the phrase *"Vay mağluplara!"* ("Woe to the vanquished!") ringing in his ears.

## 2. The city: steamboats, carriages and the view from Çamlıca

- İstanbul is experienced **from the water**. People go "by the first steamboat" to the city. Beylerbeyi has the Company's (Şirket-i Hayriye's) well-known *"dilenci" vapuru*, the "beggar" steamer that stops everywhere. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 15|Namık Kemal, *İntibah*, loc. 15]]
- **Çamlıca** is the great promenade: *"İstanbul denilen güzellikler topluluğunun içine aldığı her türlü eşsiz şeyi bir bakışta gösterecek nokta ise Çamlıca'dır."* ("The point that shows at a glance every matchless thing contained in the collection of beauties called İstanbul is Çamlıca.") On Fridays and Sundays it is crowded with the carriages of harem ladies. On other days it is empty. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 10|*İntibah*, loc. 10]], [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 13|loc. 13]]
- The Bosphorus in spring, seen from the paddle-box of a steamer: yalıs (waterside mansions), hills "enamelled" with Judas-tree blossom. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 17|Mizancı Murat, *Turfanda*, loc. 17]]
- Arriving from abroad in 1890, the hero finds his hotel's sign only in French, "Hôtel d'Amérique", with **no Turkish writing** and no prayer rug in the room. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 23|*Turfanda*, loc. 23]]
- See also the historical notes: [[İstanbul]], [[Beyoğlu and Galata]], [[Boğazlar]], [[Kadıköy and Kızıltoprak]].

## 3. The Sultan as spectacle

- The imperial barges leave Dolmabahçe as the ironclads fire salutes. Sailors shout *"Padişahım çok yaşa!"* over the roar of Armstrong guns, and even Frankish tourists wave their hats: *"Vive le Sultan!"* — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 20|*Turfanda*, loc. 20]]
- Loyalty is a public performance, a religious one and an emotional one at the same time. The hero's "patriotism and loyalty to his padishah" swell together. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 133|*Turfanda*, loc. 133]]
- Historical notes: [[Abdülhamid II]], [[Dolmabahçe Sarayı]], [[Yıldız Sarayı]].

## 4. *Alafranga* vs. *alaturka*: dress, manners and the neighbourhood's eye

- The poet Müştak Bey is *"alafrangalık meraklısı"* (a lover of European ways) and goes to the theatre in Beyoğlu at night. The night watchman mocks him: he will teach him to say *"fan-fin"*, meaning to speak Frankish. — [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 3|Şinasi, *Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 3]], [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 6|loc. 6]]
- A "newly educated" Armenian lawyer is sketched by his look: black beard, dark fez, monocle and redingote collar, *"alafrangalığa düşkünlüğü"* (fond of European ways). — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 40|*Turfanda*, loc. 40]]
- A fez-wearing Turk is mistaken for a Frank who has put on a fez. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 22|*Turfanda*, loc. 22]]
- Outrage at men in hats who laugh at a Muslim family's carriage: *"Milli ahlakımıza yazık değil mi?"* ("Isn't it a shame on our national morals?") — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 70|*Turfanda*, loc. 70]]
- The *mahalle* (neighbourhood) is a court of its own: the imam, the watchman, the sweeper and the neighbourhood coffeehouse. A house is left empty because one day in the coffeehouse someone said it was *"Uğursuzdur!"* ("It is cursed!"). — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 38|*Turfanda*, loc. 38]], [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 10|*Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 10]]

## 5. Love, marriage, the harem and the slave market

- In *Şair Evlenmesi*, marriage is arranged through a go-between (*kılavuz*) and a bride's attendant (*yenge*). The groom is tricked into marrying the older sister, and the trick is undone by **bribing the neighbourhood imam**. — [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 2|*Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 2]]
- The groom worries about the bride-price and the "face-seeing" gift (*yüz görümlüğü*): *"insan kısmının yediği bir yem var ya, adına para derler"* ("there is a feed that people eat; they call it money"). — [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 4|*Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 4]]
- **Görücü** (matchmaking visitors): *"Annemizi görücü gönderelim mi?"* ("Shall we send our mother as the matchmaker?") The ideal bride "knows French better than you" and plays the piano. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 108|*Turfanda*, loc. 108]]
- A lady steps out of her carriage in a *ferace* (a long outer cloak), with two enslaved women (*cariye*) behind her. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 19|*İntibah*, loc. 19]]
- The slave dealer (*esirci*) comes to the house to haggle over Dilâşub, a young enslaved woman. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 74|*İntibah*, loc. 74]]
- A faithful maidservant (*halayık*) who would not even hint at her wishes. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 84|*Turfanda*, loc. 84]]
- Historical note: [[Harem and the palace household]].

## 6. Money, debt and ruin

- In a single year, spending on jewellery used up properties that brought in 15,000 kuruş a month. Next the furniture went, and then even the enslaved women. The lady of the house ends up in rented corners living by needlework. The bey writes petitions for 40–60 *para* (a tiny sum) to buy a glass of rakı and a piece of bread. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 78|*İntibah*, loc. 78]]
- A poor boy from a village near Çankırı is sent to İstanbul, perhaps to become a bath attendant, and ends up in the medical school. His father is dismayed to hear the boy will study for twelve years "without earning money". — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 55|*Turfanda*, loc. 55]]
- Wartime prices, remembered bitterly in 1918: *"Neydi o üç yüz kuruşa şeker, yüz elli kuruşa et..."* ("What was that, three hundred kuruş for sugar, a hundred and fifty for meat...") — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 29|Mehmet Rauf, *Halas*, p. 29]]
- Historical notes: [[Düyun-u Umumiye]], [[National economy (Milli İktisat)]], [[Famine and epidemics]].

## 7. *Vatan*: the new word that got people exiled

- *Vatan Yahut Silistre* was first staged at Güllü Agop's Gedikpaşa Theatre on **1 April 1873**. The excitement of the audience turned into a demonstration. Namık Kemal was arrested at the theatre and exiled to Magosa (Famagusta) without trial; Ebüzziya and Ahmed Midhat were sent to Rhodes. — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 6|*Vatan Yahut Silistre*, editor's introduction, p. 6]], [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 81|p. 81]]
- The play's cry: *"Vatan! Vatan!.. Vatan tehlikede diyorum! işitmiyor musun? Beni Allah yarattı, vatan büyüttü."* ("Homeland! Homeland! I'm telling you the homeland is in danger! Don't you hear? God created me, the homeland raised me.") — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 15|*Vatan*, p. 15]]
- Homeland and faith are fused: *"bence vatan imanla beraberdir. Vatanını sevmeyen Allah'ını da sevemez."* ("to me the homeland goes together with faith. Whoever does not love his homeland cannot love his God.") — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 45|*Vatan*, p. 45]]
- The dying boy's last two words: *"Babacığım!"* ("Daddy!") and *"Vatan!"* ("Homeland!") — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 39|*Vatan*, p. 39]]
- The old sergeant's refrain in the face of every danger: *"Kıyamet mi kopar?"* ("Will the world end?") — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 51|*Vatan*, p. 51]]
- In *Turfanda*, identity is argued over: *"Müslümanların vatanı dindir"* ("the Muslims' homeland is their religion") and also *"babalarımızın Kütahyalı özbeöz Türk olduklarını"* ("that our fathers were pure Turks from Kütahya"). — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 95|*Turfanda*, loc. 95]]
- Historical notes: [[Namık Kemal]], [[Young Ottomans]], [[Silistre]], [[Crimean War (1853-1856)]], [[Ebüzziya Tevfik]], [[Ahmed Midhat Efendi]].

## 8. The new woman and the dream of education

- Zehra is cultured, knows French and plays the piano, and she becomes a **village schoolteacher**, long before *Çalıkuşu*. The editor calls it an ideal realised "only after the Republic". — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 13|*Turfanda*, editor's introduction, loc. 13]]
- *"Eksik olan yalnız maariftir. Onu yaymak o kadar güç müdür?"* ("The only thing missing is education. Is spreading it really so hard?") — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 99|*Turfanda*, loc. 99]]

## 9. The informer and the police

- In 1919–20, false *jurnal* (informer reports) put the hero of *Halas* in prison. He is charged with "spreading Bolshevik propaganda in İstanbul". — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 222|*Halas*, p. 222]], [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 230|p. 230]]
- A sleepy country police post: *"Bu ne bu be? Ne olmuş?"* ("What's this, then? What happened?") — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 210|*Halas*, p. 210]]
- Historical note: [[Informants and spies (jurnal system)]].

## 10. The end of the age: defeat, occupation, humiliation (1918–1919)

- In İzmir, after the armistice, the Frankish quarter and the Greek market are buried in Greek flags: *"Zito Venizelos, zito..."* ("Long live Venizelos!") — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 25|*Halas*, p. 25]], [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 31|p. 31]]
- *"Bu sefer sevgili Anadolu, gözbebeği İstanbul düşman çizmelerine açılıyordu."* ("This time beloved Anatolia, İstanbul the apple of our eye, was opening itself to enemy boots.") — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 40|*Halas*, p. 40]]
- A Levantine merchant's sneer, *"Vay mağluplara!"* ("Woe to the vanquished!"), makes the hero stagger "like a drunk". Everywhere he goes, *"hep düşman, hepsi düşmandı"* ("enemies everywhere, all of them enemies"). — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 60|*Halas*, p. 60]]
- The Muslim hero feels the city is shared with people he now sees as enemies: *"Bir kere memleketimiz memleket değil ki..."* ("To begin with, our country isn't even a country...") — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 26|*Halas*, p. 26]]
- İstanbul in February: muddy streets full of British lorries and French cars, with Allied officers on the Galata Bridge. — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 109|*Halas*, p. 109]]
- 15 May 1919 in İzmir, seen from a balcony: soldiers landing from boats, and men in fezzes beaten in the street. — [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 153|*Halas*, p. 153]], [[Halas (Mehmet Rauf)#p. 155|p. 155]]
- Just past the edge of the period (1920), *Reis Paşa* describes occupied İstanbul: frightened crowds on the trams, Şirket-i Hayriye steamers half empty, locals staring with hatred at British, French and Italian patrols. — [[Allahın Süngüleri Reis Paşa (Attilâ İlhan)#p. 37|Attilâ İlhan, *Reis Paşa*, p. 37]]
- Pera at night: *madams* and *mademoiselles* escorted by Allied officers, and signs in French, Greek and Armenian. — [[Allahın Süngüleri Reis Paşa (Attilâ İlhan)#p. 142|*Reis Paşa*, p. 142]]
- Historical notes: [[Armistice of Mudros (1918)]], [[Occupation of İstanbul (1918-1923)]], [[Occupation of İzmir (1919)]], [[İzmir]], [[Eleftherios Venizelos]].

## Interesting details
1. The Arnavutluk-style costume of the heroine, stretched on a floor cushion by candlelight with a book: the opening image of the 1873 stage. — [[Vatan Yahut Silistre (Namık Kemal)#p. 9|*Vatan*, p. 9]]
2. Friday and Sunday are the days for the promenade. Arranging a picnic at Çamlıca on a Tuesday makes friends laugh. — [[İntibah (Namık Kemal)#loc. 13|*İntibah*, loc. 13]]
3. The last word Şinasi wrote on his deathbed, according to the memoir printed with *Şair Evlenmesi*: *"Feskmuni"*. — [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 20|*Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 20]]
4. At Şinasi's funeral there were only a handful of people: four municipal sergeants, a quilt-maker, a relative, two typesetters from *Tasvir-i Efkâr*, the mufti and two others. — [[Şair Evlenmesi (Şinasi)#loc. 21|*Şair Evlenmesi*, loc. 21]]
5. A picnic in the hills, from the novel: lambs turning on spits, *höşmerim* (a cheese dessert) cooking, and a swing hung from a tree. — [[Turfanda mı Yoksa Turfa mı (Mizancı Murat)#loc. 163|*Turfanda*, loc. 163]]
6. The women of 1920 Ankara as imagined in *Reis Paşa*: a French tile stove, velvet curtains, a piano and rakı with chickpeas and white cheese waiting by the Pasha's armchair. — [[Allahın Süngüleri Reis Paşa (Attilâ İlhan)#p. 536|*Reis Paşa*, p. 536]], [[Allahın Süngüleri Reis Paşa (Attilâ İlhan)#p. 556|p. 556]]

## Words of the age
- ***vatan***: homeland; the charged new word of the 1870s
- ***hürriyet***: liberty
- ***millet***: nation or religious community
- ***alafranga / alaturka***: European-style vs. Turkish-style
- ***Frenk***: a Frank; any Western European
- ***mahalle***: the neighbourhood as a moral community
- ***jurnal***: an informer's report
- ***görücü***: a matchmaking visitor
- ***cariye, halayık***: enslaved or servant women of the household
- ***esirci***: slave dealer
- ***vapur***: steamboat
- ***Şirket(-i Hayriye)***: the Bosphorus ferry company
- ***Padişahım çok yaşa!***: "Long live the Sultan!"
- ***Vay mağluplara!***: "Woe to the vanquished!"
