## Summary

Otto Liman von Sanders led the [[German military mission (1913)]] formed in December 1913 to rearm and reform the Ottoman army (Murphy). He was made Inspector-General and commander of the First Army (Allawi). Because Russia objected to a German commanding a corps at [[İstanbul]], Germany made him a marshal so he could not hold a corps command, and he took the title of Inspector-General (Akşin). [[Cemal Paşa]] says Enver was first to see the harm of him holding the I Corps. His memoir *Türkiye'de Beş Yıl* (German view) is one of the vault's sources, published with notes in 1921.

The video transcripts call his mission 'the footsteps of a new fiasco'. He commanded the defence of the Straits in the [[Gallipoli Campaign (1915)]], and later commanded the [[Yıldırım Army Group]] in Palestine. In May 1916 he sent his views on sending Turkish troops to Europe to the Kaiser (Yazman). Mustafa Kemal proposed to him a withdrawal from Palestine to Aleppo (Bardakçı). Nicolle (British view) notes that even he undervalued Ottoman Arab units despite their role at Çanakkale.
