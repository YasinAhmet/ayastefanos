## Summary

İsmail Enver was the officer-hero of the 1908 revolution and war minister in the [[First World War]]. More than any other figure, he is the man the sources blame and admire for the empire's last war.

**Rise.** According to the German view ([[Anna Grosser-Rilke]]), 'the young officer Enver' stood out among the revolutionaries in Macedonia, and 'the revolution was started under Enver's leadership' ([[Young Turk Revolution (1908)]]). In the video transcripts he shouts from the balcony on 23 July 1908: 'We have cured the sick man!' Murat Bardakçı shows he was drawn into the [[Committee of Union and Progress]] together with [[Talat Paşa]]. In 1909 he was engaged to the sultan's niece [[Naciye Sultan]], and he married her in 1914. According to the American view (Timothy Childs), in Libya he drew on these ties to impose his views on older commanders ([[Italo-Turkish War (1911-1912)]]). Akşin calls him 'the hero of the [[Raid on the Sublime Porte (1913)]]', whose position in the party grew stronger after the recapture of [[Edirne]].

**War.** [[Ahmet Emin Yalman]] judges that 'only Enver Paşa imposed his will on the whole country and could drag it into the war'. He adds that Enver also held it together for four years at a price no other people would have paid. Other angles:
- [[Cavid Bey]] testified that Enver proposed sending the fleet into the Black Sea while it was still under German officers ([[Goeben and Breslau (1914)]]).
- In [[Liman von Sanders]]' German memoir, he appears as a jealous commander-in-chief.
- Kâmuran Gürün quotes his message to Talat of 2 May 1915, which frames the [[Armenian deportation (1915)]] as a response to revolt.
- David Nicolle (British view) notes that as late as July 1918 he built a new 'Army of Islam' for the Caucasus.

**Fall.** On the night of 1–2 November 1918 he fled with Talat and Cemal on a German ship (Mantran). According to Bardakçı, in 1917–18 he even met the British agent Zaharoff in Geneva. He went to Moscow and then Central Asia. [[Mustafa Kemal Atatürk]] 'never approved of Enver' and kept his men out of Anatolia ([[Kılıç Ali]]). According to the Soviet view ([[Semyon Aralov]]), news of the rout of 'Enver's band' in Central Asia made a strong impression in [[Ankara]]. He was killed in a Soviet raid in 1922; Bardakçı quotes the agent Agabekov boasting 'we killed Enver silently with sword blows'.
