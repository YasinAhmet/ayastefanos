## Summary

Prince Sabahaddin was a nephew of [[Abdülhamid II]] through his mother, and a leader of the liberal wing of the [[Young Turks]]. Allawi (Iraqi view) and Toprak both describe his programme: 'private initiative and decentralisation' (*teşebbüs-i şahsi ve adem-i merkeziyet*), taken from the conservative French thinker Le Play. It stood against [[Cavid Bey]]'s classical economics and the Unionists' centralism. Ortaylı traces his decentralism back to [[Midhat Paşa]] and forward to the 1921 constitution.

His family treated him as a traitor for organising in Europe to overthrow his uncle (Bardakçı). According to the Russian view ([[Leon Trotsky]]), it was unclear 'whether he is a not very clever amateur dreamer, or an intriguer who has not yet shown his hand'. During the [[31 March Incident (1909)]] he intervened to use the fleet against the revolt (Akşin). He was the figurehead of the opposition around [[Damat Ferit Paşa]] and [[Kamil Paşa]] ([[Freedom and Accord Party]]; Mantran).

According to the British view reported by Akşin, [[Mark Sykes]] met him in [[İstanbul]] in 1915 and heard his plan for a coup and a separate peace. In December 1918 the palace thought of turning to him, and in 1919 he was offered the leadership of Freedom and Accord. In July 1921 he sent [[Ankara]] a telegram congratulating it on its victories (Goloğlu).
