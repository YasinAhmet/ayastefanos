## Summary

Thomas Edward Lawrence, 'Lawrence of Arabia', was a British officer with the [[Arab Revolt (1916-1918)]]. Mantran (French view) calls him a man of 'enigmatic personality' among the British officers who ran the revolt. Bardakçı says the revolt was organised by him and other British agents in Cairo. In August 1917 he set up an airfield in Sinai (Murphy). Nicolle (British view) illustrates an 'Arab Army' raid. In Ottoman memory he was 'Casus Lawrence', 'Lawrence the spy', who stirred up the tribes (Talat's memoir; Grosser-Rilke). Allawi (Iraqi view) says he may have planned the killing of Abdülkadir in Damascus with [[Nuri Said]], and later worked with Churchill in the new Middle East Department.
