## Summary

Mehmed Akif (Ersoy) was the leading voice of the Islamist current in the late Empire. Mantran (French view) says he was born in 1873 in [[İstanbul]], son of a teacher at the Fatih medrese, and that he was loved by the masses: his long lyric poems spoke their bewilderment and longing, and he was ready to learn from the West. Ortaylı calls him 'a literary genius' who knew Persian poetry in all its subtlety. In the 1911 first volume of *Safahat* he mocked easy divorce under the Şeriat (Akşin). Bardakçı notes that even he hated [[Abdülhamid II]], calling him 'the owl of Yıldız'.

In the National Struggle he sat as deputy for Burdur (Kansu) and chaired the Assembly's Education Commission. When the Assembly chose a national anthem, a deputy objected that Akif himself had a poem in the contest (Goloğlu); Georgeon notes he wrote the anthem, but his attempt at an official Turkish Quran translation failed.

   > “Mehmet Akif, kitlelerin pek sevip tuttuğu bir kişiydi ve o da, uzun lirik şiirlerinde, şaşkınlık ve özlemlerini dile getiriyordu yığınların; şair, Batı'ya öyküomeye hazır ve din deyince sadece ilerlemeye engel bir şey gören Osmanlı aydınları ile İslam'daki çöküşü Batılı adetlerin hesabına yazmaya kalkma alışkanlığındaki kitleler arasındaki uçurumdan kaygılanıyordu. Mehmet Akif'e göre, İslam'ın ilerici ruhu üzerine dayanılmalıydı. Neydi izlenecek örnek?”
