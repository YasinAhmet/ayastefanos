## Summary

Lord Kitchener was British commander in Egypt ('Sirdar') and later High Commissioner there from 1911 (Akyıldız). In 1914 [[Emir Abdullah]] met him secretly in Cairo (Murphy). [[Cemal Paşa]] heard that the great Druze sheikh had been received several times by him in Cairo. Fidan records that he came to inspect on 27 November, saw no chance of progress, and the British army withdrew on 23 December. Gürün quotes the view that, when he invaded Sudan, Britain was pleading for the Armenians elsewhere. 'Kitchener's Army' was the nickname of Britain's new volunteer soldiers (Nicolle).
