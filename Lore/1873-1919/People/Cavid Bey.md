## Summary

Mehmed Cavid was the Unionists' finance minister, an economist from Salonika, and the most anti-war voice in the inner circle. With [[Rıza Tevfik]] and Ahmed Şuayib he founded a review of economics and sociology (Akşin). In July 1910 he was negotiating [[Baghdad Railway]] guarantees in London (Akyıldız).

Bardakçı notes that on the eve of war he wanted an alliance with Britain. Before the parliamentary inquiry he said he had refused to join an adventure 'that meant disaster for the country' on the day the *Goeben* and *Breslau* were sent into the Black Sea ([[Goeben and Breslau (1914)]]). He had read the German alliance text on 1 August 1914 but thought it only a draft (Yalman).

After the armistice he led the Teceddüt Party that replaced the [[Committee of Union and Progress]] (Akşin). Later he:
- served on the Public Debt council;
- advised on finance at the first Lausanne conference, where he fell out with the delegation;
- headed the 1924 [[İstanbul]] Economic Commission.

In 1926 the Independence Tribunal accused him of organising, behind the scenes, the old Unionists who met in his house. [[Kılıç Ali]] (a judge's view) writes that the 'political preparations behind the scenes were directed by Cavit Bey'. He was sentenced on 26 August 1926 and hanged in [[Ankara]] that night with [[Doktor Nazım]] and others ([[İzmir assassination plot (1926)]], [[Istanbul Independence Tribunals]]).
