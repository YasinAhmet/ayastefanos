## Summary

Kıbrıslı (the Cypriot) Mehmed Kâmil Paşa was an old Hamidian grand vizier known as 'pro-English' (Akşin). After the 1908 revolution the [[Committee of Union and Progress]] dropped [[Küçük Said Paşa]] and brought Kâmil in. Talat's memoir notes that Bulgaria's union with Eastern Rumelia and its declaration of independence happened in his time. He was ousted in 1909 and replaced by [[Hüseyin Hilmi Paşa]]. [[Faik Tonguç]], a schoolboy then, describes him visiting with Niyazi and Enver: 'a bag of bones, jaw forever moving, sunk in his armchair'.

He returned on 29 October 1912 in the Balkan disaster. Ortaylı claims the Unionists cared more about stopping his cabinet from looking victorious than about the Balkan alliance. Yalman says he was convinced Britain would not let the empire be attacked while he held power. He was overthrown in the [[Raid on the Sublime Porte (1913)]]: [[Enver Paşa]] made him write his resignation. He left for Egypt. According to Cemal, the police chief reported him slipping back into [[İstanbul]] on a Messageries Maritimes steamer shortly before Mahmud Şevket's murder.
