## Summary

The caliphate was the Ottoman sultans' claim to lead all Muslims. Some argued it passed to the Ottomans with Selim I's conquest of Egypt in 1517 (Ortaylı; see [[Hicaz]]). In the World War the Sultan-Caliph and Şeyhülislam called for jihad (Nicolle). After the sultanate's abolition in 1922, Abdülmecid became caliph only; Mustafa Kemal replied to his condolence telegram on 10 January 1923 (Bardakçı). The Assembly claimed to have held the caliphate 'in its heart' since 16 March 1920 (Goloğlu). It was abolished on 3 March 1924 (see [[Abolition of the Caliphate (1924)]]). Allawi (Iraqi view) says Hüseyin's claim to it was short-lived and fruitless.
