## Summary

Trabzon was the main Black Sea port of the east. In the mid-19th century orders banning the Georgian slave trade were sent to the governors of Trabzon, Lazistan and Canik (Badem). An 1895 provincial yearbook describes Giresun (Ortaylı). Toprak quotes complaints that foreign competitors shipped flour to Trabzon more cheaply than [[İstanbul]] merchants could. Unionist leaders passed through it into exile. Cemal Azmi, its former governor, was later assassinated (Bardakçı). In 1919–21 it was a centre of the national movement, and local quarrels were settled by Karabekir (Goloğlu).
