# The Sick Man of Europe

> **Concept** · Period: [[_Ledger 19th Century (1800-1872)|19th Century (1800-1872)]] · Found in **18** sources on **49** pages · [[Home]] · [[Timeline]]

## Summary

'The Sick Man of Europe' was Tsar Nicholas I's phrase for the [[Ottoman Empire]]. On 9 January 1853 he told the British ambassador, 'We all want the sick man to live' (video transcripts). Badem notes no one proved the 'sick man' was dying. The [[Young Turks]] hoped the constitution would heal him (Akşin), and Tonguç recalls the fear that Europe started wars so he would not recover. Fidan compares China and Turkey as the 'two sick men of the East', Turkey recovering first. Emrence notes hopes of turning the 'sick man' into 'the Japan of the Near East'.
