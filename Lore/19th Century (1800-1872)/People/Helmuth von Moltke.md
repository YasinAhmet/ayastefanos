## Summary

Helmuth von Moltke (the elder) served as a Prussian adviser in the Ottoman army in the 1830s. In 1839 he was asked to improve [[İstanbul]]'s street layout and first drew the city's map (Çelik). In 1836 he wrote that Muslim women were kept under strict watch (Akşin). He advised Hafız Paşa to attack the weak Egyptian army at the right moment (video transcripts). Hakkı Paşa quotes the view that making an army without a nation was beyond even Moltke's power. His statue stands in the German embassy's summer garden at Tarabya (Grosser-Rilke). Later Germans took him as the model of a staff chief (Hochwächter). Note: 'General Moltke' whom [[Liman von Sanders]] briefed in December 1913 was his nephew, and Reisman's Moltke is a 20th-century lawyer.
