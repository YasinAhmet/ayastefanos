## Summary

Sultan Abdülaziz reigned from 1861 to 1876. He is remembered in the sources for three things:
- **His passion for modernisation.** According to Zeynep Çelik, he would even have approved demolishing the 1,400-year-old land walls, until British antiquarians intervened.
- **His powerful navy.** According to Zafer Toprak, a strong fleet was built in his reign; under Abdülhamid it was left to rot in the Golden Horn. The video transcripts ask where 'Abdülaziz's famous navy' was during the [[Russo-Turkish War of 1877-1878]].
- **The financial collapse at the end of his reign.** Robert Mantran and Sina Akşin say he was held responsible for misgovernment and above all for the empire's bankruptcy.

He travelled to Europe in 1867. According to [[Leon Trotsky]]'s chronology he visited the [[Paris]] Exhibition, and Queen Victoria gave him the Order of the Garter. That same year a secret Bulgarian committee presented him with a draft constitution for a dual Ottoman-Bulgarian monarchy modelled on Austria-Hungary (İlber Ortaylı). According to Akyıldız and Kurşun, Egypt's İsmail Paşa won the title of Khedive by showering Abdülaziz and his mother Pertevniyal Valide Sultan with gifts.

The [[Young Ottomans]] ([[Namık Kemal]], [[Ziya Paşa]], Ali Suavi) opposed his rule. On 30 May 1876 the government of Mütercim Rüştü Paşa deposed him, and four days later he was found dead. Akşin records it as suicide. [[Murad V]] replaced him, and then [[Abdülhamid II]].
