## Summary

Ali Kemal was a journalist (*İkdam*, *Peyam-ı Sabah*), briefly interior minister under Damat Ferit in 1919, and 'one of the fiercest opponents of the national struggle and of Mustafa Kemal' (Bardakçı). The video transcripts quote his contempt for a tradesman who asked why his paper defended Armenians rather than Turks. Goloğlu notes his harsh articles denying the national struggle, while he taught at the Faculty of Letters, where he called the struggle's supporters 'cabbage leaves' and 'lapdogs'.

The Entente considered him for a government to 'bring Anatolia into line' (Akşin). According to [[Kılıç Ali]], Damat Ferit's statements implied acceptance of Armenian autonomy, and Ali Kemal was 'in a wretched state'. After the victory he wrote an article of reconciliation, which did not save him. He was lynched at [[İzmit]] on **6 November 1922** (Bardakçı). Bardakçı also notes his earlier marriage to an Englishwoman in Britain in 1903.
