## Summary

Hüseyin Rauf (Orbay) was a naval officer, the 'hero of the Hamidiye' (Bardakçı), Navy Minister at the [[Armistice of Mudros (1918)]] and later prime minister in [[Ankara]]. In 1914 he was in England to take over the dreadnought *Sultan Osman*, and he came home empty-handed when the British seized the ships (Toprak; [[Cemal Paşa]]). He was one of Cemal's fiercest opponents in the last years of the war (Bardakçı). He later called Admiral Calthorpe 'base and dishonourable' for breaking his promises.

In 1919 he joined Mustafa Kemal in Anatolia and was at [[Erzurum]] and [[Sivas]]. Mantran (French view) says he was one of the prominent men who quickly rallied to Mustafa Kemal. As a deputy in [[İstanbul]] in 1920 he was a target of the British (Gürün; Akşin). The deputies argued about whether to hand him over, and he himself created a scene with the British officers at the door (Kansu; Akşin). In [[Ankara]] he chaired the Council of Ministers. He received congratulations after the victory of 1922 (Goloğlu). In the Russian view (Aralov) he was nervous and suspicious about Soviet aims on the Straits. Güneş notes his speeches defending the Sultan's position and the caliphate. Later he lived abroad and served as ambassador in London (Bardakçı).
