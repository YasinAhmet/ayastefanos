## Summary

Faysal bin Hüseyin was the son of [[Şerif Hüseyin]], leader of the Arab Northern Army, later king of Syria and then of Iraq. Allawi's *Faisal I of Iraq* (Iraqi view) is his main biography in the vault. [[Cemal Paşa]] sent him towards Medina in 1916 before the revolt. In December 1916 he led his army to Yenbu (Murphy). His forces took Akaba on 6 July 1917 and raided the railway (Mantran). Sanders (German view) notes the growing activity of Arab units under him east of the Jordan. He also mentions an offer from Faysal that the Turkish side did not trust.

After the war he became king of Syria. A British report of July 1919 says he sent a letter of loyalty to Vahdettin (Akşin), and he protested to Curzon about the armistice lines. The Clemenceau–Faysal agreement of 1920 ended the pan-Arab dream (Emrence), and the French drove him out (Bardakçı). Britain then made him king of Iraq. Allawi records his modest lifestyle and constant deficits. He died in 1933 after an appendix operation in Switzerland (Bardakçı); [[Cemal Paşa]]'s editor mentions claims he was poisoned. His grandson Faysal II was killed in the 1958 Iraqi coup (Akşin). Aralov (Russian view) recalls Mustafa Kemal telling an envoy of 'Sultan' Faysal, 'I am not a sultan'.
