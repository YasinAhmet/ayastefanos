## Summary

Ali Rıza Paşa was grand vizier from October 1919 to March 1920, between two Damat Ferit governments. The [[Sivas]] congress ([[Heyet-i Temsiliye]]) forced Ferit out, and Ali Rıza's cabinet was the national movement's partner. Akşin notes it was no real change of policy: he himself had served in the previous government. His war minister, Mersinli [[Cemal Paşa]], telegraphed that the cabinet 'shared the views of the Heyet-i Temsiliye' ([[Kılıç Ali]]). Goloğlu calls the cabinet incompetent, with some ministers even hostile to the movement. Squeezed by the palace, the Entente and [[Ankara]], he resigned on 3 March 1920 (Akşin), just before the occupation of [[İstanbul]].

Earlier he had been a general: commander of the Western Army facing Serbs, Greeks and Montenegrins in the [[Balkan Wars (1912-1913)]] (Durgun). Hochwächter notes his cholera. A different Ali Rıza Paşa commanded east of the Jordan in 1918 ([[Liman von Sanders]]). Ali Rıza al-Rikabi led [[Faysal]]'s party in Damascus (Allawi).
