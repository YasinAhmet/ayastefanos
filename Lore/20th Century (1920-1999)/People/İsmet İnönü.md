## Summary

İsmet (İnönü) was a staff officer who became commander of the Western Front, chief negotiator at Lausanne and later prime minister. As a student at the War Academy he followed the Russo-Japanese War with excitement (video transcripts). With [[Enver Paşa]] and [[Hafız Hakkı Paşa]] he appears in an early group photograph (Hakkı Paşa). On 1 June 1919 he wrote to [[Kâzım Karabekir]] about the dark days and the gilded pill of a 'mandate' (Goloğlu). Before joining [[Ankara]] he was told by the [[Heyet-i Temsiliye]] that leaning to Britain would help British plans (Akşin).

He won the first victory over the Greeks at İnönü near [[Eskişehir]] (İnan; Mantran, French view, calls him a great soldier and a stubborn, supple diplomat). He was commander of the Western Front at Kocatepe on 25 August 1922 (Argun). He left Lausanne on 6 February 1923 (Sorguç). Aralov (Russian view) quotes his notes to the Allies in [[İstanbul]]. Later he was prime minister (1936) and was pressed by Churchill to enter the Second World War (Akşin). Ortaylı's judgement: not the greatest marshal, diplomat or statesman, but remarkable for a man in his thirties.
