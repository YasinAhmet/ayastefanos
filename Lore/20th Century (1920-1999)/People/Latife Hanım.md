## Summary

Latife Hanım, daughter of the [[İzmir]] merchant Uşakizade Muammer Bey, became the wife of [[Mustafa Kemal Atatürk]]. After the capture of İzmir in 1922 she ran the household of the mansion where the commander-in-chief stayed (Goloğlu). Grosser-Rilke (German view) knew her as a remarkable girl of fifteen. [[Kılıç Ali]] says the Gazi decided to take her as his life companion. Aralov (Russian view) says that, following Mustafa Kemal's advice, she worked among [[Ankara]] women to free them from 'feudal remnants'. A visitor to Ankara found the couple living simply (Fidan). Akşin's volume includes an essay on 'Latife's gesture' and Eastern and Western feminisms. The divorce came abruptly: Mustafa Kemal had not slept all night, and he announced it through two lines to the [[Anadolu]] Ajansı (Kılıç Ali).
