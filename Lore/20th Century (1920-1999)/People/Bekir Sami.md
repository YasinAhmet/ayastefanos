## Summary

Bekir Sami (Kunduh) was a former governor of Beirut and Aleppo. Ortaylı says that as governor of Beirut he was sympathetic to the Zionist leaders, and Baron Edmond de Rothschild left him large donations for Muslim schools and charities (Akşin). He was at the [[Sivas]] Congress and on the [[Heyet-i Temsiliye]]. As [[Ankara]]'s first Foreign Minister he left for Moscow on 11 May 1920; the Soviets accepted the National Pact on 3 June (Akşin). At the London Conference (February–March 1921) he made a strong impression (Goloğlu). But he also signed separate agreements with the Allies, which Ankara rejected, and Franklin-Bouillon later argued over what he had agreed (Gürün). [[Kılıç Ali]] points out that Nutuk lists him among the mandate supporters. (A different Colonel Bekir Sami commanded the division at [[Bursa]].)
