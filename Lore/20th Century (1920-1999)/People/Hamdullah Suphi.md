## Summary

Hamdullah Suphi (Tanrıöver) was an orator and head of the [[Türk Ocağı]]. Akşin tells how young nationalists met him on the ferry and chose him for the movement; he then joined the Türk Ocağı and presided over it for years. On 11 June 1913 he wrote that the *Türk Yurdu* movement was 'a naked and hungry child' (Yalman). Toprak notes his lectures for women at the Ocak during the 1918 loan campaign. Under the pen-name 'Toplu İğne' he wrote satirical pieces in *Davul* (Akşin).

In 1920 it was his proposal that deputies from the dissolved Istanbul parliament go to [[Ankara]] for the Assembly of 23 April (Güneş). In the Assembly he wrote a declaration 'to the whole Islamic world' (Akşin), and read [[Mehmed Akif]]'s poem from the rostrum as the national anthem (Goloğlu). [[Kılıç Ali]] says a famous telegram was written by him and signed by Mustafa Kemal. According to the Russian view (Aralov), Mustafa Kemal liked the ideas that he and Ahmet Ağaoğlu gave him for reforms.
