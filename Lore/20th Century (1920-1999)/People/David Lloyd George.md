## Summary

David Lloyd George was British Prime Minister in 1916–22. He admired [[Eleftherios Venizelos]] and his hostility to Turkey was stated in the Commons (Bardakçı). In May 1919 the Supreme Council of Lloyd George, Clemenceau and Wilson gave [[İzmir]] to Greece (Mantran). He never published the Bristol report on Greek misdeeds at İzmir (Gürün). According to the Russian view (Aralov), he offered Greece [[İstanbul]] and a cross on Hagia Sophia to bring her into the war. At the London Conference of 1921 he wanted to talk to the Istanbul delegates, but [[Tevfik Paşa]] gave the right to speak to [[Ankara]]'s (Goloğlu). The Chanak crisis of September 1922 ended his premiership (video transcripts; Akşin). Akşin concludes he was certainly anti-Turkish, though how pro-Greek he was is less clear.
