# Mustafa Sabri Efendi

> **Person** · Period: [[_Ledger 20th Century (1920-1999)|20th Century (1920-1999)]] · **Dates:** 1869–1954 ⚠ Not from vault sources (from my general knowledge; not found in the quoted pages) · Found in **14** sources on **78** pages · [[Home]] · [[Timeline]]

## Summary

Mustafa Sabri Efendi was a religious scholar and deputy for Siirt, and Şeyhülislam (chief religious official) in Damat Ferit's governments. According to Georgeon, in 1909 he backed the revolution and explained constitutional rule to medrese students in *Beyanü'l-Hak*. He later became the voice of conservative, anti-modernist Islamism (Mantran; Akşin). He sat on the central committee of the [[Freedom and Accord Party]] (June 1919). The editor of Argun's diary counts him among the founders of the pro-British [[English Friendship Society]].

As Şeyhülislam, according to the video transcripts, he at first refused a fatwa on the execution of the Boğazlıyan governor, saying it was a judicial matter, then yielded. He entered Ferit's fourth cabinet on 5 April 1920. That cabinet's fatwas condemned the nationalists (see [[Internal revolts (1919-1921)]]). Bardakçı notes that in exile he fell out with [[Şerif Hüseyin]], who stopped him writing in favour of the Ottoman caliphate.
