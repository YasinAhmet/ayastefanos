## Summary

Damat Ferit Paşa was a son-in-law of the dynasty, leader of the [[Freedom and Accord Party]] (founded 21 November 1911, per Childs), and five times grand vizier in 1919–20 under [[Mehmed VI Vahdettin]]. He stands as the face of collaboration with the occupiers. According to the Chinese travellers' view quoted by Fidan, Ferit was 'the leader of the pro-British', who replaced the pro-German [[Young Turks]].

What he did in 1919–20:
- **Against the national movement.** On 20 July 1919, under British pressure, he ordered [[Mustafa Kemal Atatürk]] and [[Rauf Orbay]] to be arrested wherever they were found (Goloğlu). He sent Ali Galip to break up the [[Sivas Congress (1919)]] (Akşin).
- **Against the Unionists.** He set up the court-martial 'of Nemrut Mustafa' that tried them ([[Unionist trials (1919-1920)]]; see [[Nemrut Mustafa Paşa]]).
- **Returning to power.** He came back in April 1920, launching the civil war and signing [[Treaty of Sèvres (1920)]].

Mustafa Kemal, telegraphing on 15 September 1919, accused him of keeping the nation from its beloved padishah (Bardakçı). His government even collected and destroyed Mustafa Kemal's book *Zabit ve Kumandan ile Hasbihal*. According to the Soviet view ([[Semyon Aralov]]), 'neither the sultan nor Damat Ferit had conscience or love of country'. [[Afet İnan]]: he 'never knew the world, Turkey, the Turkish nation'. Bardakçı quotes a dynasty member's verdict on the 1924 exile: 'We were ruined because of Damat Ferit.'
