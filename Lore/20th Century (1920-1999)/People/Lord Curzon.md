## Summary

Lord Curzon was British Foreign Secretary after the war. Earlier, as Viceroy of India, his first act was to assert British power in the Persian Gulf (Akyıldız). In 1919–20 he warned against rewarding Damat Ferit's compliance too cheaply (Akşin). Allawi (Iraqi view) quotes his promise to [[Faysal]] that 'England wishes to help you with all her strength'. The video transcripts quote him blaming Lloyd George's hatred of the Turks and 'mad love' for the Greeks for the crisis. In October 1922 he went to [[Paris]] to agree with the Allies on the evacuation of Thrace (Goloğlu). On 6 October he replied to the Sultan's request about guarantees for his safety (Bardakçı). At Lausanne he was the chief British delegate, and on 4 February 1923 he handed İsmet his own draft treaty (Akşin). Aralov (Russian view) records claims that he incited France over the capitulations. His nephew Colonel Rawlinson was in [[Erzurum]] in 1919 (Kansu).
