## Summary

Ali Fethi (Okyar) was a Unionist officer and diplomat, and a lifelong friend of [[Mustafa Kemal Atatürk]]. As military attaché in [[Paris]], then commander at Kesriye, he was brought into the Committee (Bardakçı). In Libya in 1911–12 he and [[Enver Paşa]] were known as fiery [[Young Turks]] (Childs, American view). As ambassador in Sofia he wrote to [[Cemal Paşa]] (letter of 19 December 1913, Bardakçı). After the armistice he and Mustafa Kemal published a paper in [[İstanbul]] (Kemal).

In [[Ankara]] he was Interior Minister. Aralov (Russian view) reports rumours that he met Townshend in Beirut. His cabinet programme covered army reforms (Güneş). Atatürk later asked him to found a new opposition party, which Fethi 'accepted gladly as a national duty', with Atatürk promising financial help ([[Kılıç Ali]]). Later he asked to be ambassador in London. After 1938 he was among the 'estranged' men brought back into parliament (Akşin).
