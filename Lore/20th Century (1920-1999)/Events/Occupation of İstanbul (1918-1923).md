## Summary

The victors occupied İstanbul in practice from November 1918, and officially from March 1920 (Bardakçı). What Sultan Vahdettin feared came on 16 March 1920, when the city was formally occupied (Bardakçı). By 10 in the morning most places were taken and many people arrested for Malta, and martial law was declared (Akşin; see [[Malta exiles (1919-1921)]]). The Grand National Assembly later declared that it had held the caliphate 'in its heart' since 16 March 1920 (Goloğlu). The British High Commissioner De Robeck judged the government's conduct after 16 March (Akşin). See [[İstanbul]] and [[Opening of the Grand National Assembly (1920)]]. (Several quotes extracted under this entity only share the date '16 March' of other years.)
